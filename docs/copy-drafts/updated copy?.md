## Method page: The Cultural Read

**Headline:** How we know what will work.

**Intro:** Before we design anything, we do what a good cultural researcher does: we look closely at a place and the people who give it meaning. We call this the Cultural Read. It takes about two weeks, and everything we make afterwards rests on it.

**Four questions we answer**

1. **Who is really here?** Not the customer you describe in a pitch, but the people who walk in: their habits, their occasions, what they order and why they return.
2. **What does the place mean to them?** A venue can be a first-date room, a family table, a late-night refuge or a neighbourhood landmark. Meaning shapes loyalty more than price does.
3. **What does the venue signal today?** We read your website, your menu, your signage, your reviews and how you appear online as a guest would, and note where they agree and where they disagree.
4. **Where is the gap?** The distance between what the place is and what it appears to be. This is where the work lives.

**What you receive from it**

A one-page statement of the gap, a plain-language reading of your audience, and a short list of decisions it makes easier: what to say, what to show, what to stop doing.

**A note on theory**

The Cultural Read draws on ideas from cultural sociology and the study of creative industries, including how places build reputation, how taste signals belonging, and how memory attaches to rooms and objects. You will never need to read any of it. We translate it into choices you can act on.

## Private notes (not for the site)

**If an owner asks whether you run their social media**

Short reply, in person or by message:

> We don't run accounts, but we make the brand, the templates and the photography direction, so whoever posts for you has something strong to post from.

If they want it taken further, offer one of these:

- **Templates only:** a set of post and story templates, a colour and type guide and a photography shot list, handed over for them or their staff to use. Quote it as a one-off project.
- **A referral:** name one freelancer you trust who runs accounts. Do not promise a fee or a handover you have not arranged.
- **A pilot, only if you choose to try it:** a fixed number of weeks, a fixed number of posts, a written end date, and a price that reflects how much you dislike it. Never an unwritten extra inside another package.

**Rule for yourself:** do not say "I'll see what I can do" to a paying client. Say "let me come back to you by Friday", decide calmly, then answer with one of the options above.

**Before you quote any retainer:** the Monthly Partner is now menus, event print, website upkeep and copy. Confirm there is enough real monthly work for the venue before you agree to £900 a month.
