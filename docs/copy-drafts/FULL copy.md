## Method page: The Cultural Read

**Headline:** How I know what will work.

**Intro:** Before I design anything, I do what a good cultural researcher does: I look closely at a place and the people who give it meaning. I call this the Cultural Read. It takes about two weeks, and everything I make afterwards rests on it.

**Four questions I answer**

1. **Who is really here?** Not the customer you describe in a pitch, but the people who walk in: their habits, their occasions, what they order and why they return.
2. **What does the place mean to them?** A venue can be a first-date room, a family table, a late-night refuge or a neighbourhood landmark. Meaning shapes loyalty more than price does.
3. **What does the venue signal today?** I read your website, your menu, your Instagram, your signage and your reviews as a guest would, and note where they agree and where they disagree.
4. **Where is the gap?** The distance between what the place is and what it appears to be. This is where the work lives.

**What you receive from it**

A one-page statement of the gap, a plain-language reading of your audience, and a short list of decisions it makes easier: what to say, what to show, what to stop doing.

**A note on theory**

The Cultural Read draws on ideas from cultural sociology and the study of creative industries, including how places build reputation, how taste signals belonging, and how memory attaches to rooms and objects. You will never need to read any of it. I translate it into choices you can act on.
